## Use capacity and cash, not advertised rates

These are arithmetic scenarios, calculated on 5 October 2026, not market rates or promises. A month is approximated as 52/12 weeks. Utilization includes the effect of sales, administration, revisions, and idle time.

| Available hours/week | Billable share | Billable hours/month | Price/hour | Gross/month if all billed work is paid |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 20 | 50% | 43.3 | $50 | $2,167 |
| 30 | 60% | 78 | $50 | $3,900 |
| 40 | 60% | 104 | $30 | $3,120 |

`gross = available hours/week × 52/12 × billable share × realized hourly rate`

For fixed-price work, realized hourly rate is **collected project revenue divided by all project hours**, including scoping, calls, revisions, and support. Also track collected revenue divided by **all business hours** so selling time is not invisible.

Three $1,000 projects are $3,000 gross only if all are paid in the relevant period. Under an illustrative 5% payment/platform cost and $200 monthly expenses, $3,000 collected gross leaves $2,650 before income tax. Reaching $3,000 before income tax in that scenario needs about $3,368 gross. These costs are assumptions; use the actual payment route. A take-home target also needs jurisdiction-specific tax estimates.

## One offer, one proof package

Choose the owner's strongest demonstrable system. Start with a **bounded reliability improvement to an existing Roblox game**, rather than promising an entire combat, inventory, trading, and live-ops stack at one generic price.

Candidate offer: diagnose and fix one reproducible saving, duplication, or server-validation problem in a test copy, with a reproduction, regression check, and handover. Only offer this if the owner can demonstrate the skill. If another specialty has stronger evidence, use that instead.

Before quoting, write down the failing behavior, supported environment, acceptance check, access needed, exclusions, estimated hours, revision allowance, delivery date, and support limit. An investigation with unknown scope is a separately capped discovery milestone. Do not promise a security guarantee or production-data access.

Use one case study the owner has permission to publish. Show the problem, their role, before/after behavior, a short demonstration, and one meaningful test. Use verified results only. Reuse existing shipped work before building four new demos. A technical explanation and private walkthrough can substitute when client code cannot be published.

## Ten working days to evidence

The hour caps below are **proposed allocation limits**, not estimates of guaranteed completion. Adjust to the owner's actual hours. Pause additional broad research during this sprint.

| Work | Cap | Deliverable |
|---|---:|---|
| Clarify eligibility, capacity, target, and strongest proof | 1 hour | A short private decision record |
| Prepare one case study and offer | 5 hours | Reviewable proof, scope, and draft pitch |
| Verify an initial focused list of buyers or live roles | 3 hours | Up to 10 qualified entries; record fewer if fewer qualify |
| Review replies, scope discussions, or application feedback | 4 hours | Measured objections and next actions, after owner-approved contact |
| Review results and choose one adjustment | 1 hour | Continue, narrow, or change the offer |

A qualified lead has a current public opening or credible relevant need, a legitimate organization, an eligible geography/engagement type, a source URL checked on a stated date, and an appropriate contact/application route. Do not label old salary examples as live leads. The original studio list is a research starting point.

Draft message structure, for owner review only:

> I specialize in [verified specialty]. In [work I can name], I [specific verified contribution], demonstrated here: [proof]. Your [current opening or stated problem] looks relevant. I can deliver [bounded outcome and acceptance check]. If useful, I can scope the work before quoting.

No invented metrics, unspecified claims of sole authorship, or blanket mass messages. Contact, applying, posting, and account creation still require the owner's explicit OK.

## Decision rules

- **No qualified leads:** investigate eligibility or channel choice before making more portfolio assets.
- **Qualified leads but no replies after an owner-approved batch:** inspect delivery, fit, and the pitch. A small batch is weak evidence, not proof the entire market is bad.
- **Replies but no scoped opportunities:** ask what is missing: proof, availability, trust, price, or relevance. Change one variable next.
- **Opportunities but no acceptance:** compare scope, commercial terms, and alternatives. Do not automatically cut price.
- **Paid work but poor effective rate:** tighten scope and acceptance criteria before taking more work.
- **Payment or approval delayed:** it is pipeline, not collected income. Track expected payment dates separately.

Do not wait six weeks to discover that the offer has no traction. Also do not jump immediately to a new language or platform on one inconclusive batch.

## Weekly scoreboard

Record qualified leads checked, owner-approved contacts/applications actually sent, replies, qualified discussions, proposals, paid starts, collected USD, unpaid invoices and due dates, delivery hours, and total business hours. Empty cells mean unknown, not zero. Keep client-confidential details outside the public repository.

For now, game work gets only a small pre-agreed experiment budget of time. No ad spending, store, daily reward system, or long content schedule before a playable core earns a second round of effort.
